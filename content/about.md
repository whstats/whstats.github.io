**Notes on Statistics** is a public working notebook on mathematical statistics and the probability tools behind it.

The site is intentionally small. It does not try to reproduce a textbook. Instead, each page isolates one reusable idea and answers five questions:

1. What is the result really controlling?
2. Which assumptions make that control possible?
3. What is the shortest route from setup to conclusion?
4. Which example makes the scaling visible?
5. Where will the result be used next?

## Reading level

The notes assume familiarity with probability, linear algebra, calculus, and basic asymptotic notation. Proof sketches keep the decisive step and omit routine algebra.

## Conventions

- Random variables are defined on a common probability space unless stated otherwise.
- Constants are displayed when they clarify scale; convention-dependent constants are identified explicitly.
- Convergence and norm notation follows the [[notation|Notation]] page.
- Internal links mark genuine mathematical dependencies, not merely related vocabulary.

## Corrections

These pages are living notes. A claim should be treated as provisional if its assumptions are not stated next to it.
